## About The Planet Crafter

The Planet Crafter is a first-person space survival game where you play as a convict sent to terraform an uninhabitable planet. The core gameplay loop centers on mining minerals, crafting machines, and gradually transforming the hostile environment into a livable world with oxygen, heat, pressure, and eventually a complete biosphere.

**Key Gameplay Mechanics:**
- Extensive mining system with Ore Extractors (T1, T2, T3)
- 13 different mineable ores scattered across the planet
- Terraformation stages from barren wasteland → Flora → Trees → Animals
- Meteor showers replenish surface minerals
- Cave systems with specialized mineral deposits
- Progressive unlocking of rare minerals as terraformation advances

**Complete Mineral List in Game:**
1. **Basic/Common Minerals** (available everywhere):
   - Iron (Fe)
   - Cobalt (Co)
   - Silicon (Si)
   - Magnesium (Mg)
   - Titanium (Ti)
   - Ice (H₂O)

2. **Specialized/Rare Minerals** (specific locations):
   - Aluminum (Al) - Aluminum Hills biome
   - Sulfur (S) - Sulfur Fields
   - Iridium (Ir) - Iridium Cave
   - Uranium (U) - Uranium deposits
   - Osmium (Os) - Osmium Cave
   - **Zeolite** - Zeolite Cave (Flora stage unlock)
   - Super Alloy (composite material, requires Super Alloy Cave)

3. **Special Resources:**
   - Pulsar Quartz (craftable, requires zeolite)
   - Obsidian (volcanic regions)

## Exhibition Specimens - The Zeolite Family

### Why Zeolites Matter in The Planet Crafter

Zeolite is **the most unique mineral** in The Planet Crafter because:
1. It's the **only ore** that doesn't fall during meteor showers
2. It's **locked behind terraformation progress** - unavailable until Flora stage (700 MTi)
3. It has a **dedicated cave system** filled with spectacular white crystalline formations
4. It's **critical for late-game progression** - required for DNA Manipulators, Gas Extractors, and Pulsar Quartz
5. It represents a **real mineral family** rather than a single element

In gameplay terms, zeolite marks the transition from basic survival to advanced biological terraformation. You can't create trees or manipulate DNA without it.

## Specimen 1: Natrolite

**Mineral Name:** Natrolite  
**Chemical Formula:** Na₂Al₂Si₃O₁₀·2H₂O  
**Specimen ID:** #23925  
**Type:** gem  
**Crystal System:** Orthorhombic  
**Hardness:** 5-5.5  
**Color:** Colorless, white, sometimes yellowish or reddish  

### Mineralogy

Natrolite is a zeolite mineral forming fibrous, radiating needle-like crystals. It's a sodium aluminum silicate hydrate, part of the zeolite group characterized by open framework structures with channels and cavities. The name comes from "natrium" (Latin for sodium).

Natrolite forms in cavities within basalts and other volcanic rocks during low-temperature hydrothermal alteration. The fibrous crystals often radiate from a central point in spray-like clusters called "sprays" or "sheaves."

### In The Planet Crafter

Natrolite represents the zeolite family in the game. In the **Zeolite Cave**, massive white crystalline formations dominate the landscape - these are meant to evoke natrolite's characteristic white, fibrous appearance. Players mine zeolite using T2 or T3 Ore Extractors placed anywhere in the Zeolite Cave.

Zeolite becomes available once the terraformation reaches the **Flora stage** (700 MTi terraformation index). At this point, zeolite crystals emerge from the ground all across the planet, pushed up by tree roots in a beautiful game mechanic that ties mineralogy to biological growth.

### Real-World Applications

- **Water purification** - Zeolites' porous structure traps contaminants
- **Ion exchange** - Water softeners use zeolites to remove calcium
- **Catalysis** - Petroleum refining (zeolite catalysts crack hydrocarbons)
- **Aquariums** - Removes ammonia from fish tanks
- **Agriculture** - Soil amendments, slow-release fertilizers
- **Nuclear waste** - Zeolites can trap radioactive cesium and strontium
- **Desiccants** - Industrial drying agents (molecular sieves)
- **Gas separation** - Separates oxygen from nitrogen in air

### Fun Facts

- Natrolite shows perfect cleavage parallel to its length, breaking into needle-like fragments
- When natrolite dehydrates (loses water), it can cause the crystals to crack audibly!
- The three-dimensional channel structure of zeolites inspired the design of artificial molecular sieves
- **In The Planet Crafter:** Players need zeolite to craft the DNA Manipulator, which is hilariously appropriate - zeolites' molecular sieving is conceptually similar to DNA filtering!
- The Zeolite Cave is one of the most beautiful locations in the game, with glowing white crystals amid blue fog

## Specimen 2: Heulandite (with Quartz in Basalt)

**Mineral Name:** Heulandite  
**Chemical Formula:** (Ca,Na)₂₋₃Al₃(Al,Si)₂Si₁₃O₃₆·12H₂O  
**Specimen ID:** #76597  
**Type:** rock (black heulandite with quartz in basalt matrix)  
**Crystal System:** Monoclinic  
**Hardness:** 3.5-4  
**Color:** White, colorless, pink, red, brown (black variant is rare)  

### Mineralogy

Heulandite is one of the most common zeolite minerals, named after British mineral collector Henry Heuland. It forms distinctive coffin-shaped or tabular crystals. Like all zeolites, it contains water molecules in its crystal structure that can be driven off by heating and reabsorbed by cooling.

**Black Heulandite** is particularly unusual - the dark color comes from volcanic glass inclusions or manganese impurities. Finding heulandite with **quartz** in **basalt** shows the typical volcanic cavity (vesicle) environment where these minerals form together during late-stage hydrothermal activity.

**Formation Context:**  
When basaltic lava cools, gas bubbles create cavities (vesicles). Later, hot mineral-rich fluids percolate through these cavities, depositing zeolites like heulandite and silica minerals like quartz. This is a classic zeolite occurrence.

### In The Planet Crafter

This specimen represents the **geological context** of zeolite formation in The Planet Crafter. The game's Zeolite Cave features:
- White crystalline zeolite formations (like heulandite crystals)
- Basaltic host rock (the cave walls)
- Association with other minerals (the game shows zeolite near osmium, sulfur, and other ores)

The **basalt connection** is perfect for a planet terraforming game - basaltic volcanism is common on rocky planets (Mars, the Moon, Mercury). The game's volcanic biome and cave systems reflect realistic planetary geology.

### Real-World Applications

- **Radiation shielding** - Heulandite can absorb and immobilize radioactive cesium
- **Ion exchange resins** - Water softening and purification
- **Molecular sieves** - Industrial separation processes
- **Agricultural amendments** - Slow-release fertilizers and water retention
- **Aquaculture** - Removes ammonia from fish farming systems
- **Cat litter** - Natural zeolites control odor and moisture
- **Nuclear cleanup** - Used after Fukushima to remove radioactive materials from water

### Fun Facts

- Heulandite loses 10% of its weight when heated to 350°C as water evaporates from its channels!
- The coffin-shaped crystals are so distinctive that heulandite is easily identified even by beginners
- **In The Planet Crafter:** The association with basalt is geologically accurate - most terrestrial planets have basaltic surfaces, making zeolites plausible for alien worlds
- Iceland (a basaltic volcanic island) is famous for spectacular zeolite specimens
- Some heulandites show reversible color changes when hydrated/dehydrated

## Specimen 3: Stellerite (in Basalt)

**Mineral Name:** Stellerite  
**Chemical Formula:** Ca₄(Si₂₈Al₈)O₇₂·28H₂O  
**Specimen ID:** #54359  
**Type:** rock (in basalt matrix)  
**Crystal System:** Orthorhombic  
**Hardness:** 3.5-4  
**Color:** White, colorless, pale yellow, pink  

### Mineralogy

Stellerite is a calcium-rich zeolite forming distinctive fan-shaped or wheat-sheaf crystal aggregates. Named after Georg Wilhelm Steller, the naturalist who explored Kamchatka and Alaska in the 1740s (famous for discovering Steller's sea cow).

Stellerite is rarer than other zeolites and highly prized by collectors for its delicate, radiating crystal sprays. It's closely related to stilbite but has a different water content and forms orthorhombic crystals instead of monoclinic.

**Formation:** Like other zeolites, stellerite forms in basalt vesicles during low-temperature hydrothermal alteration. The fan-shaped crystal habit is diagnostic.

### In The Planet Crafter

Stellerite represents the **diversity within the zeolite family**. While the game simply calls it "zeolite" (without specifying which species), the exhibition shows that **zeolites are actually a large family** of related minerals with varied crystal forms:
- Natrolite: needle-like radiating sprays
- Heulandite: coffin-shaped tablets
- Stellerite: fan-shaped sheaves

All three form in the same volcanic environment (basalt cavities), all have similar chemistry (aluminum-silicon frameworks with water), and all have the same industrial uses.

### In-Game Zeolite Uses

In The Planet Crafter, zeolite is essential for:
1. **Gas Extractor** (extracts atmospheric gases)
2. **DNA Manipulator** (creates tree seeds from plant samples)
3. **Pulsar Quartz** (zeolite + uranium + super alloy → pulsar quartz for fusion reactors)
4. **Nuclear Fusion Generator** (requires pulsar quartz)
5. **Tree Spreaders** (advance terraformation to Trees stage)

The game correctly associates zeolites with **gas separation** and **molecular manipulation** - real zeolites do exactly this in industrial applications!

### Real-World Applications

Same as other zeolites:
- Molecular sieves (gas/liquid separation)
- Catalysis (petroleum cracking, chemical synthesis)
- Ion exchange (water softening, radioactive waste cleanup)
- Adsorbents (drying agents, odor control)
- Soil amendments and fertilizers

**Specific to stellerite:**
- Collector specimens (rare, beautiful crystal forms)
- Museum displays
- Research into zeolite crystal growth mechanisms

### Fun Facts

- Stellerite is named after a German naturalist who died on a deserted Bering Sea island while exploring Russia's far east
- The wheat-sheaf crystal habit makes stellerite specimens look like miniature grain bundles
- **In The Planet Crafter:** The game's zeolite appearing during the Flora stage (when plants emerge) creates a poetic connection - stellerite looks like wheat, which appears when the planet becomes verdant
- Kamchatka, Russia (where Steller worked) produces some of the world's finest zeolite specimens
- Stellerite can contain up to 28 water molecules per formula unit - nearly half its weight is water!

## Exhibition Educational Themes

### 1. **Mineral Families vs. Single Species**

The zeolite group contains **over 40 natural species** and **200+ synthetic varieties**. The three specimens show:
- Different crystal systems (orthorhombic vs. monoclinic)
- Different cations (sodium, calcium)
- Different crystal habits (needles, tablets, fans)
- **Same core chemistry:** Framework silicates with channels

**Lesson:** "Zeolite" isn't one mineral - it's a family, like "feldspar" or "garnet"

### 2. **Volcanic Mineralogy & Planetary Science**

All three specimens formed in **basalt**, the most common volcanic rock in the solar system:
- **Earth:** Oceanic crust, volcanic islands
- **Moon:** Mare (dark plains)
- **Mars:** Volcanic plains, Olympus Mons
- **Mercury:** Smooth plains
- **Venus:** 90% of surface

**Lesson:** If you terraform a rocky planet, you'll likely encounter zeolites in basaltic rocks

### 3. **Porous Minerals & Technological Applications**

Zeolites' framework structure with channels and cavities enables:
- **Water purification** (traps contaminants)
- **Gas separation** (molecular sieving)
- **Catalysis** (petroleum refining)
- **Nuclear waste cleanup** (ion exchange)

**The Planet Crafter connection:** Gas Extractors and DNA Manipulators use zeolite in-game because zeolites really do filter molecules in real life!

### 4. **Hydrated Minerals in Space Exploration**

Zeolites contain **up to 20% water** by weight in their crystal structure:
- Potential water source for astronauts
- Indicators of past water on Mars
- Important for understanding planetary history
- Can be "baked out" and reused

**Lesson:** Finding zeolites on another world would indicate past liquid water activity

### 5. **Video Game Geology Accuracy**

The Planet Crafter gets zeolite geology **remarkably right**:
- ✅ Forms in caves (volcanic cavities)
- ✅ Associated with basaltic host rock
- ✅ White, crystalline appearance
- ✅ Late-stage formation (Flora stage = late terraformation)
- ✅ Used for gas extraction (real zeolites separate gases)
- ✅ Linked to biological processes (DNA Manipulator → molecular sieving analogy)

**Lesson:** Some games actually research their mineralogy!

### Discussion Questions

- Why would a survival game feature zeolites specifically?  
  *Answer: Zeolites filter water and gases - perfect for terraforming!*

- How many different zeolites are in the exhibition?  
  *Answer: Three - they're a family of related minerals, not just one*

- Where would you find zeolites on Mars?  
  *Answer: In ancient volcanic regions where hot water once flowed*

- What percentage of a zeolite is water?  
  *Answer: Up to 20% by weight - it's a hydrated mineral*

**The Planet Crafter** is **NOT** in the "hockey stick" (D/R/S/E only) category:
- **13 different mineable ores** (high diversity)

- Ore extraction mechanics based on real mining engineering
- Terraformation stages unlock minerals (like real planetary evolution)

**Position on diversity spectrum:** HIGH geological diversity
