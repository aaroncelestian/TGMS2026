## The "Hockey Stick" Pattern

Based on your poster analysis, these 4 minerals appear in the vast majority of games:
1. **Diamond** (GTA, potentially others)
2. **Ruby** (GTA, potentially others)
3. **Sapphire** (Diablo, potentially others)
4. **Emerald** (Minecraft, potentially others)

These "hockey stick" minerals are virtually inescapable in video game design and represent the games with the lowest mineralogical diversity but highest market success.

## Exhibition Statistics

**Mineralogical Diversity:**
- 68.8% of games use D/R/S/E only or fictional minerals (Low Diversity)
- 17.8% have moderate real mineral diversity (Medium)
- 13.4% show high geological diversity (High)

**Your Exhibition:**
- Average: 6.5 minerals per game
- Range: 2 (Elder Scrolls) to 10 (Minecraft)
- Total unique minerals: 30+
- Total specimens: 81

**Key Insights:**
- Most games cluster in the "blade" (low diversity, high market success)
- Games like Minecraft show that geological diversity CAN coexist with mass appeal
- Your museum specimens span the full range from "hockey stick" gems to rare collector minerals

## Educational Value

**What visitors learn:**
1. Mineralogy basics (crystal systems, hardness, composition)
2. Real-world mineral uses vs. game representations
3. Geological context (how minerals form)
4. Cultural significance (ancient uses, modern applications)
5. Rarity and value (both scientific and economic)

**Unique angle:**
Games are a $220B industry (bigger than music + movies combined). By connecting video game engagement with real mineralogy, this exhibition meets young people where they are and transforms casual gaming knowledge into genuine scientific interest.
