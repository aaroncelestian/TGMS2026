## Astroneer

### 1. Sphalerite (Specimen #24230)

**Formula:** (Zn,Fe)S

**Description:** Zinc sulfide, the primary ore of zinc. When transparent, sphalerite can be faceted into brilliant gems with exceptional fire (dispersion). In Astroneer, it represents valuable mineral resources.

**Fun Fact:** Faceted sphalerite has higher dispersion (fire) than diamond! However, it's rarely used in jewelry because it's too soft and cleaves easily.

---

### 2. Sphalerite Crystal (Specimen #13517)

**Formula:** (Zn,Fe)S

**Description:** Natural sphalerite crystals showing the characteristic tetrahedral form. The name comes from Greek 'sphaleros' meaning treacherous, because it was easily confused with other minerals.

**Fun Fact:** Sphalerite is the primary source of zinc, which is used to galvanize steel! About 13 million tons of zinc are produced from sphalerite each year.

---

### 3. Titanite (Sphene) (Specimen #13808)

**Formula:** CaTiSiO₅

**Description:** Calcium titanium silicate with exceptional brilliance and fire. Also called sphene. In Astroneer, titanite represents titanium-bearing minerals valuable for space technology.

**Fun Fact:** Titanite has higher dispersion than diamond, creating incredible rainbow flashes! It's a collector's gemstone because it's too soft for everyday jewelry.

---

### 4. Titanite (Faceted) (Specimen #33361)

**Formula:** CaTiSiO₅

**Description:** Faceted titanite showing its exceptional optical properties. The high refractive index and strong dispersion create a gemstone with remarkable brilliance.

**Fun Fact:** The name 'sphene' comes from the Greek word for wedge, referring to the crystal shape. It was renamed 'titanite' to reflect its titanium content.

---

### 5. Titanite Crystal (Specimen #48552)

**Formula:** CaTiSiO₅

**Description:** Natural titanite crystal showing the characteristic wedge or envelope shape. Forms in igneous and metamorphic rocks, often associated with feldspar and amphibole.

XXX **Fun Fact:** Titanite is found in rocks on Earth, the Moon, and in meteorites - making it one of the few minerals we've identified across multiple worlds.

## Baldur's Gate

### 1. Black Opal (Specimen #8114)

**Formula:** SiO₂·nH₂O

**Description:** Black opal is the rarest and most valuable type of opal, displaying brilliant flashes of color against a dark body tone. In Baldur's Gate, opals are valuable gems used in trading and enchantments.

**Fun Fact:** Black opals from Lightning Ridge, Australia can be worth more per carat than diamonds! The play-of-color is caused by microscopic silica spheres diffracting light.

---

### 2. Black Opal (Cabochon) (Specimen #21894)

**Formula:** SiO₂·nH₂O

**Description:** Polished black opal cabochon showing intense play-of-color. The dark background makes the color flashes more dramatic and valuable.

**Fun Fact:** Opals contain up to 20% water! They can crack if they dry out too quickly, which is why jewelers sometimes store them in water.

---

### 3. Black Opal in Matrix (Specimen #12244)

**Formula:** SiO₂·nH₂O

**Description:** Black opal in its natural host rock (ironstone matrix). Shows how opal forms in sedimentary environments, filling cavities and cracks.

XXX**Fun Fact:** Silica-rich groundwater infiltrates buried fossils, dissolving the original material and depositing opal in its place - like creating a mineral cast that preserves even microscopic details of ancient life.

---

### 4. Turquoise (Specimen #13737)

**Formula:** CuAl₆(PO₄)₄(OH)₈·4H₂O

**Description:** Copper aluminum phosphate prized for thousands of years for its distinctive blue color. In Baldur's Gate, turquoise appears as a semi-precious gem used in trade and crafting.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient Egyptians mined turquoise in the Sinai Peninsula over 5,000 years ago! The name comes from 'Turkish stone' because it was traded through Turkey.

---

### 5. Turquoise (Rough) (Specimen #16770)

**Formula:** CuAl₆(PO₄)₄(OH)₈·4H₂O

**Description:** Natural turquoise with host rock matrix, often showing brown or black veining. The matrix patterns are highly valued in Native American jewelry.

**Fun Fact:** Turquoise can change color over time! Body oils, cosmetics, and sunlight can alter its blue color to green.

---

### 6. Purple Garnet (Specimen #33334)

**Formula:** Mg₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Rare purple variety of pyrope garnet. In Baldur's Gate, garnets are valuable gems associated with protection and vitality in D&D lore.

**Fun Fact:** Purple garnets are extremely rare! Most garnets are red, and the purple color comes from trace amounts of chromium and vanadium.

---

### 7. Purple Garnet (Faceted) (Specimen #33431)

**Formula:** Mg₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Faceted purple garnet showing excellent clarity and color. The high refractive index of garnet gives it exceptional brilliance when cut.

**Fun Fact:** Garnets have been used as gemstones for over 5,000 years! Ancient Egyptians used them as inlays in jewelry and carvings.

## Diablo

### 1. Yellow Topaz (Specimen #51749)

**Formula:** Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂

**Description:** Natural yellow topaz, one of the hardest gemstones. In Diablo, topaz gems are socketed into equipment to provide lightning resistance and magic find bonuses.

**Fun Fact:** Imperial topaz (golden yellow to pink) is the most valuable topaz variety and was named after the Russian royal family who claimed exclusive rights to it!

---

### 2. Yellow Topaz Crystal (Specimen #25424)

**Formula:** Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂

**Description:** Natural topaz crystal showing the characteristic orthorhombic form. Topaz forms in igneous rocks and pegmatites, often as large, well-formed crystals.

**Fun Fact:** The largest cut topaz in the world is the Brazilian Princess, weighing 21,327 carats (about 9.4 pounds)!

---

### 3. Sapphire (Specimen #37438)

**Formula:** Al₂O₃

**Description:** Blue variety of corundum, second only to diamond in hardness. In Diablo, sapphires provide cold resistance and are essential for high-level crafting.

**Fun Fact:** The Star of India, one of the world's largest star sapphires at 563 carats, was stolen from the American Museum of Natural History in 1964 and later recovered!

---

### 4. Sapphire Crystal (Specimen #76800)

**Formula:** Al₂O₃

**Description:** Natural sapphire crystal showing the hexagonal barrel shape typical of corundum. The blue color comes from trace amounts of iron and titanium.

XXX **Fun Fact:** Sapphires come in every color except red.  When it's red corundum is called ruby! There are pink, yellow, green, and even colorless sapphires.

---

### 5. Sapphire in Matrix (Specimen #24306)

**Formula:** Al₂O₃

**Description:** Sapphire crystals in their natural host rock, typically marble or metamorphic schist. Shows the geological context of sapphire formation.

**Fun Fact:** Kashmir sapphires, considered the finest in the world, come from a remote region in the Himalayas and have a unique velvety blue color!

## Dwarf Fortress

### 1. Rock Crystal (Quartz) (Specimen #23608)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Pure, colorless quartz crystal, also called rock crystal. In Dwarf Fortress, quartz is one of many minerals with realistic geological properties and uses.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient civilizations believed rock crystal was ice frozen so hard it would never melt! The word 'crystal' comes from the Greek 'krystallos' meaning ice.

---

### 2. Quartz Crystal Cluster (Specimen #45196)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Natural quartz crystal cluster showing the characteristic hexagonal prisms with pyramidal terminations. Quartz is the second most abundant mineral in Earth's crust.

**Fun Fact:** Quartz makes up about 12% of Earth's land surface! It's found in almost every geological environment and rock type.

---

### 3. Serpentine (Specimen #52415)

**Formula:** (Mg,Fe)₃Si₂O₅(OH)₄

**Description:** Hydrous magnesium silicate with a characteristic snake-skin appearance. In Dwarf Fortress, serpentine is valued for its workability and attractive green color.

**Fun Fact:** Serpentine is California's state rock! It forms when oceanic crust is altered by water, and it's often found in areas with tectonic activity.

## Elite Dangerous

### 1. Benitoite (Specimen #13791)

**Formula:** BaTiSi₃O₉

**Description:** Rare barium titanium silicate, the official state gem of California. Found in only a few locations worldwide, primarily San Benito County, California. Shows strong blue fluorescence under UV light.

**Fun Fact:** Benitoite is rarer than diamond! Gem-quality material comes almost exclusively from one mine in California.

---

### 2. Benitoite (Large Crystal) (Specimen #63278)

**Formula:** BaTiSi₃O₉

**Description:** Large gem-quality benitoite crystal. Under UV light, benitoite fluoresces bright blue-white, a spectacular identifying feature. Crystals over 1 carat are extremely rare.

**Fun Fact:** When the California mine closed in 2005, benitoite prices skyrocketed. It's considered one of the rarest gemstones on Earth!

---

### 3. Benitoite in Natrolite (Specimen #22245)

**Formula:** BaTiSi₃O₉ in Na₂Al₂Si₃O₁₀·2H₂O

**Description:** Benitoite crystals in natrolite (a zeolite mineral). This is the typical occurrence - benitoite forms in veins with serpentine, natrolite, and neptunite. The white natrolite makes the blue benitoite stand out dramatically.

**Fun Fact:** The blue benitoite, white natrolite, and neptunite (which shows reddish colors under bright light) together are sometimes called 'patriotic minerals' for their red, white, and blue colors!

---

### 4. Rutile (Synthetic) (Specimen #8203)

**Formula:** TiO₂

**Description:** Synthetic titanium dioxide grown for industrial and decorative use. Natural rutile is typically red-brown, but synthetic versions can be made in many colors. Has higher dispersion (fire) than diamond.

**Fun Fact:** Synthetic rutile was used as a diamond substitute in the 1940s-50s but has 6x the 'fire' of diamond, making it look fake!

---

### 5. Rutile (Twinned Crystal) (Specimen #51838)

**Formula:** TiO₂

**Description:** Natural rutile forming characteristic elbow-shaped or knee-shaped twins. Rutile often forms golden needle-like inclusions in quartz (rutilated quartz) highly valued by collectors.

**Fun Fact:** Rutilated quartz with golden rutile needles is called 'Venus hair stone' or 'Cupid's darts' in ancient folklore!

---

### 6. Rutile (Natural Crystal) (Specimen #19705)

**Formula:** TiO₂

**Description:** Natural rutile crystal showing metallic luster. Rutile is the primary ore of titanium, the 9th most abundant element in Earth's crust. Essential for aerospace alloys and white pigment.

XXX**Fun Fact:** Almost all modern white paint uses titanium dioxide from rutile. It's the whitest, brightest pigment known!

## Final Fantasy

### 1. Pink Beryl (Morganite) (Specimen #37446)

**Formula:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈

**Description:** Pink variety of beryl colored by manganese, named after banker J.P. Morgan. Part of the beryl family that includes emerald and aquamarine. In Final Fantasy, pink crystals often represent love or healing magic.

**Fun Fact:** Morganite was discovered in Madagascar in 1910. Tiffany & Co.'s gemologist named it after J.P. Morgan who was a major gem collector!

---

### 2. Pink Beryl (Crystal) (Specimen #6663)

**Formula:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈

**Description:** Natural morganite crystal showing characteristic hexagonal form of the beryl family. The beryl structure makes it durable and brilliant when cut.

**Fun Fact:** Some morganites are heated to deepen their color—a practice accepted in the gem trade!

---

### 3. Rubellite (Pink Tourmaline) (Specimen #8279)

**Formula:** (Ca,K,Na)(Al,Fe,Li,Mg,Mn)₃(Al,Cr,Fe,V)₆(BO₃)₃(Si,Al,B)₆O₁₈(OH,F)₄

**Description:** Red to pink variety of tourmaline. True rubellite maintains its color in all lighting conditions. Tourmaline has the most complex chemistry of any silicate mineral. In FF, represents fire or passion elements.

XXX **Fun Fact:** Tourmaline is pyroelectric—when heated or cooled, it develops an electrical charge on its surface that attracts dust and ash. Dutch traders in the 1700s noticed this property and used tourmaline crystals to clean ash from their pipes.

---

### 4. Rubellite in Matrix (Specimen #65675)

**Formula:** Complex tourmaline in pegmatite

**Description:** Rubellite tourmaline crystals in pegmatite host rock. Pegmatites are the source of most gem tourmalines, formed from water-rich granite magmas.

**Fun Fact:** Brazil's Paraíba tourmalines (electric blue-green) can sell for over $20,000 per carat!

---

### 5. Danburite (Specimen #33346)

**Formula:** CaB₂Si₂O₈

**Description:** Calcium borosilicate mineral, often colorless with diamond-like brilliance. Named after Danbury, Connecticut where it was first found. Rare in gem quality. In FF, could represent light or holy magic.

**Fun Fact:** Danburite has higher dispersion (fire) than quartz, making cut stones very brilliant! It's one of the more 'undervalued' gemstones.

---

### 6. Electrum (Specimen #54651)

**Formula:** Au-Ag (natural alloy)

**Description:** Natural alloy of gold and silver (20-80% gold, 20-80% silver). Forms naturally when both metals deposit together. Ancient civilizations used it for coins. In Final Fantasy, electrum is a mid-tier metal for equipment.

**Fun Fact:** The first metal coins in history (600 BCE in Lydia, now Turkey) were made of electrum! The ratio of gold to silver varied, causing economic problems.

## Grand Theft Auto

### 1. Diamond (Jewelry) (Specimen #22703)

**Formula:** C

**Description:** Pure carbon crystallized under extreme pressure and temperature. The hardest natural material on Earth. In GTA, diamonds are the ultimate heist prize worth millions.

**Fun Fact:** The GTA Online Casino Heist diamond haul would weigh about 5 pounds in real life!

---

### 2. Diamond (Rough) (Specimen #55030)

**Formula:** C

**Description:** Uncut diamond crystal showing natural octahedral form. Most diamonds require cutting to reveal their brilliance. Only about 20% of mined diamonds are gem quality.

**Fun Fact:** Diamonds are 1-3 billion years old, formed 100+ miles deep in Earth's mantle, then brought to the surface by explosive volcanic eruptions!

---

### 3. Diamond (Large Rough) (Specimen #55747)

**Formula:** C

**Description:** Large uncut diamond crystal. The biggest diamonds can be worth tens of millions. The Cullinan Diamond (3,106 carats rough) was cut into 9 major stones for the British Crown Jewels.

**Fun Fact:** The Lesedi La Rona diamond, found in 2015, was 1,109 carats (the size of a tennis ball) and sold for $53 million!

---

### 4. Diamond (Crystal) (Specimen #55748)

**Formula:** C

**Description:** Perfect octahedral diamond crystal. This geometric form is the natural shape of many diamonds. The most valuable diamonds are colorless (D-grade) or fancy colored (blue, pink, red).

XXX**Fun Fact:** The Hope Diamond (45.52 carats, deep blue) is said to be cursed!

---

### 5. Diamond in Kimberlite (Specimen #24217)

**Formula:** C in ultramafic rock

**Description:** Diamond crystal in its original host rock (kimberlite). Kimberlite pipes are ancient volcanic vents that brought diamonds from the mantle to Earth's surface at supersonic speeds.

**Fun Fact:** Kimberlite eruptions are so violent (faster than the speed of sound) they haven't occurred in recorded human history!

---

### 6. Ruby (Specimen #22714)

**Formula:** Al₂O₃

**Description:** Red variety of corundum (aluminum oxide) colored by chromium. One of the four precious stones along with diamond, emerald, and sapphire. In GTA, rubies represent luxury wealth.

XXX **Fun Fact:** The Sunrise Ruby (25 ct) sold for $30 million in 2015, making it the most expensive ruby ever. Large, fine rubies are rarer than comparable diamonds!

---

### 7. Ruby Crystal (Specimen #63577)

**Formula:** Al₂O₃

**Description:** Natural ruby crystal showing characteristic hexagonal form. Myanmar (Burma) produces the finest 'pigeon blood' red rubies. Ruby is the second-hardest natural gemstone after diamond.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient Hindus called ruby 'ratnaraj' (king of precious stones) and believed it protected warriors in battle!

## Minecraft

### 1. Calcite (Specimen #24562)

**Formula:** CaCO₃

**Description:** Calcium carbonate, one of Earth's most common minerals. Forms massive limestone deposits and spectacular crystals. In Minecraft, calcite generates naturally in geodes and amethyst formations.

**Fun Fact:** Calcite is so common that it makes up about 4% of Earth's crust! The white cliffs of Dover are made almost entirely of calcite.

---

### 2. Calcite (Crystal) (Specimen #12144)

**Formula:** CaCO₃

**Description:** Calcite exhibits incredible crystal variety with over 800 different crystal forms. Shows perfect rhombohedral cleavage and strong double refraction (birefringence).

**Fun Fact:** Clear calcite (Iceland spar) was used by Vikings for navigation—it can show the position of the sun even on cloudy days!

---

### 3. Lapis Lazuli (Specimen #22630)

**Formula:** (Na,Ca)₈(AlSiO₄)₆(S,SO₄,Cl)₂

**Description:** Actually a rock (not a single mineral) composed mainly of lazurite with calcite and pyrite. Prized for thousands of years for its intense blue color. In Minecraft, it's essential for enchanting.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient Egyptians ground lapis lazuli to make eyeshadow, and Renaissance painters paid more for ultramarine blue pigment than for gold!

---

### 4. Lapis Lazuli (Rock) (Specimen #21561)

**Formula:** (Na,Ca)₈(AlSiO₄)₆(S,SO₄,Cl)₂

**Description:** Lapis lazuli rock specimen showing the characteristic deep blue color with golden pyrite flecks. This metamorphic rock forms in contact zones.

**Fun Fact:** Lapis lazuli has been mined in Afghanistan's Badakhshan province for over 6,000 years, making it one of the oldest mining operations in history!

---

### 5. Lapis Lazuli (Rough) (Specimen #12344)

**Formula:** (Na,Ca)₈(AlSiO₄)₆(S,SO₄,Cl)₂

**Description:** Lapis in its natural state shows the complex mixture of lazurite, calcite (white), and pyrite (gold). Forms in marble during contact metamorphism.

**Fun Fact:** The best lapis lazuli comes from Afghanistan, where it's been mined for over 6,000 years!

---

### 6. Emerald (Specimen #22630)

**Formula:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈

**Description:** Green variety of beryl colored by chromium and/or vanadium. One of the 'big four' precious stones. In Minecraft, emeralds are the villager trading currency.

**Fun Fact:** Emeralds are actually rarer than diamonds! Most natural emeralds contain inclusions called 'jardin' (French for garden) that look like tiny plants.

---

### 7. Emerald in Matrix (Specimen #37596)

**Formula:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈

**Description:** Emerald crystals growing in their host rock (matrix), typically mica schist or carbonate rocks. Shows how emeralds naturally occur in metamorphic environments.

XXX **Fun Fact:** Most emeralds form in metamorphic rocks like mica schist. Colombian emeralds are exceptional—they crystallized from hydrothermal fluids moving through sedimentary black shales, a geologically unique setting!

---

### 8. Emerald Crystal (Specimen #63596)

**Formula:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈

**Description:** Natural emerald crystal showing the characteristic hexagonal prismatic form of beryl. The green color comes from trace amounts of chromium and vanadium.

**Fun Fact:** Emeralds are 20 times rarer than diamonds in nature, making them one of the most valuable gemstones!

---

### 9. Obsidian (Specimen #35460)

**Formula:** SiO₂ (volcanic glass)

**Description:** Natural volcanic glass formed when lava cools too quickly for crystals to form. In Minecraft, obsidian is created when water touches lava source blocks and is the gateway to the Nether.

XXX**Fun Fact:** Obsidian can be knapped into blades sharper than surgical steel - with edges just a few molecules thick. Some surgeons experimented with obsidian scalpels for delicate procedures in the past, though they never became standard practice.

---

### 10. Gold (Specimen #63417)

**Formula:** Au

**Description:** Native gold, one of the few metals found pure in nature. Extremely malleable, ductile, and resistant to corrosion. In Minecraft, gold tools are fast but weak.

**Fun Fact:** All the gold ever mined in human history would fit in a cube about 22 meters on each side! One ounce of gold can be beaten into a sheet covering 300 square feet.

## Return to Moria

### 1. Sunstone (Specimen #74535)

**Formula:** (Ca,Na)(Al,Si)₄O₈

**Description:** Feldspar with metallic inclusions (usually hematite or copper) that create a glittering effect called aventurescence. In Return to Moria, sunstone provides light in the dark depths.

**Fun Fact:** Vikings may have used sunstone (Iceland spar calcite) for navigation, but the aventurescent feldspar sunstone we know today was discovered in Oregon!

---

### 2. Sunstone (Cabochon) (Specimen #74536)

**Formula:** (Ca,Na)(Al,Si)₄O₈

**Description:** Polished sunstone cabochon showing the characteristic metallic schiller effect. The copper platelets inside the feldspar create a warm, glowing appearance.

**Fun Fact:** Oregon sunstone is unique because it contains copper inclusions, while most other sunstones contain iron-based minerals!

---

### 3. Sunstone Carving (Specimen #74532)

**Formula:** (Ca,Na)(Al,Si)₄O₈

**Description:** Carved sunstone object showcasing the material's unique optical properties. The metallic inclusions create a warm, sun-like glow when light passes through.

**Fun Fact:** The largest sunstone ever found in Oregon weighed over 5 pounds and was discovered in 2011!

---

### 4. Iolite (Cordierite) (Specimen #33389)

**Formula:** (Mg,Fe)₂Al₄Si₅O₁₈

**Description:** Magnesium aluminum silicate showing strong pleochroism—appears different colors when viewed from different angles. Also called 'water sapphire' or 'Viking's compass.'

**Fun Fact:** Vikings may have used iolite's pleochroism to navigate on cloudy days—it can show the position of the sun even when it's hidden!

---

### 5. Citrine (Specimen #17000)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Yellow variety of quartz colored by iron. Natural citrine is rare; most commercial citrine is heat-treated amethyst. In Moria, citrine represents the wealth of the dwarven kingdom.

**Fun Fact:** The name 'citrine' comes from the French word 'citron' meaning lemon! Natural citrine is much paler than the deep orange heat-treated variety.

---

### 6. Citrine Crystal (Specimen #7190)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Natural citrine crystal showing the hexagonal prismatic form typical of quartz. Forms in igneous and metamorphic rocks, often in geodes and pegmatites.

**Fun Fact:** The largest faceted citrine in the world is the Malaga, weighing 20,200 carats (about 8.8 pounds)!

## Stardew Valley

### 1. Malachite (Specimen #24460)

**Formula:** Cu₂CO₃(OH)₂

**Description:** A copper carbonate hydroxide mineral prized for its distinctive green banded patterns. In Stardew Valley, malachite is donated to Gunther's museum and can be used in crafting.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient Egyptians ground malachite into powder for eye shadow and used it in amulets for protection.

---

### 2. Neptunite (Specimen #20200)

**Formula:** KNa₂Li(Fe²⁺,Mn²⁺)₂Ti₂Si₈O₂₄

**Description:** A rare titanosilicate mineral forming prismatic crystals. Named after Neptune, the Roman god of the sea, due to its oceanic associations in California localities.

**Fun Fact:** Neptunite is almost exclusively found in a few locations in California, making it a highly sought-after collector's mineral.

---

### 3. Celestine (Celestite) (Specimen #22263)

**Formula:** SrSO₄

**Description:** A strontium sulfate mineral named for its often celestial blue color. Forms beautiful tabular or prismatic crystals in sedimentary environments.

**Fun Fact:** The red color in emergency flares and fireworks comes from strontium extracted from celestine.

---

### 4. Baryte (Specimen #47121)

**Formula:** BaSO₄

**Description:** A barium sulfate mineral, unusually heavy for a non-metallic mineral. The name comes from the Greek 'barys' meaning 'heavy.'

**Fun Fact:** You've probably swallowed baryte if you've ever had a barium x-ray examination at a hospital!

---

### 5. Baryte (Desert Rose) (Specimen #76060)

**Formula:** BaSO₄

**Description:** Rosette-shaped baryte crystal clusters that form in arid environments, often incorporating sand grains. These 'desert roses' are actually baryte, not gypsum as commonly believed.

**Fun Fact:** Desert roses form when baryte crystallizes in sandy soil during evaporation in arid climates.

---

### 6. Kyanite (Specimen #25333)

**Formula:** Al₂SiO₅

**Description:** An aluminum silicate polymorph with anisotropic hardness—harder along one axis than perpendicular to it. Usually forms blade-like blue crystals.

**Fun Fact:** Kyanite has different hardness values depending on the direction you scratch it—4.5 in one direction, 7 in the other!

## The Witcher

### 1. Black Pearl (Specimen #44229)

**Formula:** CaCO₃ (biogenic)

**Description:** Organic gem produced by mollusks, colored by dark organic compounds. Natural black pearls are extremely rare, mainly from Tahiti. In The Witcher, pearls are valuable crafting components and currency.

**Fun Fact:** Natural black pearls can sell for $10,000-$20,000! Most 'black pearls' in jewelry are actually dyed.

---

### 2. Pearl (Pink Fingers) (Specimen #40910)

**Formula:** CaCO₃ (biogenic)

**Description:** Baroque pearl with unusual finger-like protrusions. Baroque pearls are irregularly shaped, each one unique. Pink pearls get their color from the mollusk's mantle tissue.

**Fun Fact:** Baroque pearls were once considered defective but are now prized for their unique organic shapes!

---

### 3. Pearl (White Blister) (Specimen #40914)

**Formula:** CaCO₃ (biogenic)

**Description:** Blister pearl that formed attached to the mollusk's shell rather than free-floating. These pearls have a flat back and domed front. Often used in mabe pearl jewelry.

**Fun Fact:** Cleopatra supposedly dissolved a pearl in wine and drank it to prove Egypt's wealth to Marc Antony!

---

### 4. Pearl (Abalone Tooth) (Specimen #40925)

**Formula:** CaCO₃ (biogenic)

**Description:** Pearl from abalone mollusk showing spectacular iridescent colors. Abalone pearls are extremely rare (1 in 100,000 abalone) and display colors matching the interior of abalone shells.

**Fun Fact:** Abalone pearls can show blue, green, purple, pink, and silver simultaneously! A large one can be worth $100,000+.

### 5. Pearl (Pink Conch) (Specimen #40907)

## World of Warcraft

### 1. Peridot (Specimen #33350)

**Formula:** (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄

**Description:** Gem-quality olivine, one of the few gemstones that occurs in only one color. Forms in the Earth's mantle and arrives via volcanic activity or meteorites. In WoW, used in jewelry crafting.

**Fun Fact:** Peridot is found in meteorites! Ancient Egyptians called it the 'gem of the sun' and believed it protected against nightmares.

---

### 2. Peridot (Olivine Crystal) (Specimen #65655)

**Formula:** (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄

**Description:** Olivine crystals showing natural form. Olivine is one of the first minerals to crystallize from cooling magma and is a major component of Earth's upper mantle. The gem variety is called peridot.

**Fun Fact:** Olivine makes up most of the upper 250 miles of Earth. It's also found on the Moon, Mars, and in comets!

### 3. Garnet (Spessartine) (Specimen #33365)

**Formula:** Mn₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Spessartine (spessartite) garnet, the manganese-aluminum variety. Named after Spessart, Germany. Shows vibrant orange colors. In WoW, garnets are versatile gems for various stat bonuses.

**Fun Fact:** The famous 'Mandarin garnets' from Namibia are spessartines so bright orange they revolutionized the gem market in the 1990s!

### 4. Garnet (Spessartine) - Faceted (Specimen #33319)

**Formula:** Mn₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Cut and polished spessartine garnet showing brilliant fire and color. High-quality spessartines from Nigeria and Namibia are among the most sought-after garnets.

**Fun Fact:** Spessartine garnets can show a color-change effect under different lighting, appearing more red in incandescent light!

### 5. Garnet (Spessartine) - Large Crystal (Specimen #33401)

**Formula:** Mn₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Large natural spessartine crystal. Garnets form 12-sided or 24-sided crystals (dodecahedrons and trapezohedrons). The cubic crystal system produces these distinctive shapes.

**Fun Fact:** Garnet crystals are so well-formed they were used as ball bearings in early machinery!

### 6. Garnet (in Schist) (Specimen #25288)

**Formula:** Mn₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃

**Description:** Garnet crystals embedded in mica schist, their typical metamorphic host rock. This shows how garnets naturally occur during regional metamorphism of sedimentary rocks.

**Fun Fact:** Geologists use garnets as 'geothermometers' to calculate the temperature and pressure conditions during metamorphism!

### 7. Ametrine (Specimen #23941)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Natural bicolored quartz containing both amethyst (purple) and citrine (yellow) in the same crystal. Occurs naturally only in Bolivia's Anahi mine. The color zoning results from different oxidation states of iron during crystal growth.

**Fun Fact:** Legend says the Anahi mine was given as a dowry when a Spanish conquistador married a Bolivian princess in the 1600s!

## The Legend of Zelda

### 1. Opal (Specimen #20561)

**Formula:** SiO₂·nH₂O

**Description:** Hydrated silica forming colorful internal diffraction patterns. Precious opal displays 'play-of-color' from light diffraction through silica spheres. In Zelda games, opals are valuable gems used for selling or upgrading.

**Fun Fact:** Opals can contain up to 20% water! In Breath of the Wild, opals are worth 60 rupees each.

---

### 2. Opal (Fire Opal) (Specimen #33174)

**Formula:** SiO₂·nH₂O

**Description:** Fire opal is a transparent to translucent opal with warm body colors ranging from yellow through orange to red. May or may not show play-of-color. Mexico is the primary source.

**Fun Fact:** Ancient Romans believed opals brought good luck, while some cultures thought they could make you invisible!

---

### 3. Amethyst (Specimen #33446)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Purple variety of quartz, colored by iron and natural irradiation. The most valuable quartz variety. In Zelda, amethyst is often a mid-tier valuable gemstone.

**Fun Fact:** The name 'amethyst' comes from Greek 'amethystos' meaning 'not intoxicated'—ancient Greeks believed it prevented drunkenness!

---

### 4. Amethyst Cluster (Specimen #55690)

**Formula:** SiO₂

**Description:** Natural amethyst forming crystal clusters (geodes). These spectacular formations occur when quartz crystallizes in gas cavities within volcanic rocks. The deepest purple is most valuable.

**Fun Fact:** The largest amethyst geode ever found weighs over 13,000 pounds and is displayed in Australia. If you heat amethyst, it turns yellow (citrine)!
